Understanding Cloud Computing

Learn how cloud technology is transforming the way applications and services are built.

A

Alok Jain

Oct 15, 2026 · 10 min read

Not long ago, running an application meant buying physical servers, housing them somewhere safe, and maintaining them yourself. Cloud computing changed that completely — today, anyone can rent computing power by the minute from providers like AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud.

The three layers of cloud service

Cloud computing is usually split into three models. IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service) gives you raw virtual machines and storage — you manage everything above that. PaaS (Platform as a Service) handles the operating system and runtime, so you just deploy your code. SaaS (Software as a Service) is a finished product you simply use, like Gmail or Notion.

Most companies use a mix of all three, depending on how much control versus convenience a particular task needs.

Why scalability changed the game

Before the cloud, handling a traffic spike meant physically buying and installing more servers — a process that took weeks. Cloud platforms let you scale up or down in seconds, automatically, based on real demand. This is what makes flash sales, viral apps, and global services possible without crashing.

"The cloud didn't just make computing cheaper — it made computing elastic, and that changed what founders were willing to build."

What this means for developers

Understanding cloud fundamentals is no longer optional for backend or full-stack developers. Concepts like load balancing, containers, and serverless functions all build directly on top of these cloud service models.

Starting with a free tier on any major provider is the fastest way to actually understand these ideas instead of just reading about them.
